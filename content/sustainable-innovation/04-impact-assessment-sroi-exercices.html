Exercice du cours — refaire le calcul

Electric cars for patients — SROI

Investment €100,000. Outcomes (pedagogical assumptions): patients’ health 60 × €1,000 (deadweight 20%, attribution 20%, 2 yrs) · appointments 80 × €500 (15%, 10%, 2 yrs) · caregivers 50 × €600 (25%, 20%, 2 yrs) · facilities 120 × €100 (10%, 10%, 1 yr). Displacement 0% everywhere; drop-off 20% per year; discount rate 3.5%. Compute the SROI.

Voir la correction

1. Year-1 net = gross × (1 − deadweight) × (1 − attribution): 60,000 × 0.8 × 0.8 = 38,400 · 40,000 × 0.85 × 0.9 = 30,600 · 30,000 × 0.75 × 0.8 = 18,000 · 12,000 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 9,720 → 96,720 (corrigé du cours).

2. Year 2 (drop-off 20%; facilities last 1 year only): 38,400 × 0.8 + 30,600 × 0.8 + 18,000 × 0.8 = 30,720 + 24,480 + 14,400 = 69,600.

3. Discounting at 3.5%: 96,720 / 1.035 = 93,449 ; 69,600 / 1.035² = 64,968 (slide; exact value 64,972) → PV = 158,417.

4. NPV = 158,417 − 100,000 = 58,417. SROI = 158,417 / 100,000 = 1.58 : 1.

5. Interpretation: each €1 invested generates about €1.58 of discounted social value (pedagogical scenario).

Payback (calculé, optional step of the course): after year 1 cumulated PV = 93,449 < 100,000; remaining 6,551 on year 2’s 64,968 → payback ≈ 1 + 6,551 / 64,968 ≈ 1.10 years (using the course’s simple formula “time for cumulative discounted benefits to match the investment”, assuming benefits spread evenly across year 2).

Exercices d’entraînement

Exercice d’entraînement généré

A — Output, outcome or impact?

Classify: (1) a repair service completes 2,000 repairs; (2) customers keep their garments longer and buy fewer new ones; (3) a lasting reduction of resource consumption and emissions linked to product turnover.

Voir la correction

(1) Output: what the organization produces, observable and measurable. (2) Outcome: change for users (behaviour). (3) Impact: broader, lasting change for society/environment (this wording follows the clothing example of the course).

Exercice d’entraînement généré

B — Which deduction?

Name the SROI deduction: (a) a training programme claims all job placements, but the region’s natural exit rate from unemployment is high; (b) a lighting scheme cuts crime locally while neighbouring areas see more crime; (c) other organisations also support the same people on the same outcome; (d) the value of an outcome falls each year after the programme.

Voir la correction

(a) Deadweight (would have happened anyway; baseline = natural exit rate). (b) Displacement. (c) Attribution. (d) Drop-off.

Exercice d’entraînement généré

C — Full SROI calculation

A programme costs €50,000. One outcome: 40 beneficiaries × €800 proxy, deadweight 25%, attribution 20%, displacement 0%, lasting 2 years with 10% drop-off per year. Discount rate 3.5%. Compute the SROI and say whether the programme creates more social value than it costs.

Voir la correction

1. Données: investment 50,000 ; gross = 40 × 800 = 32,000 ; deadweight 25%, attribution 20%, displacement 0%, drop-off 10%, r = 3.5%.

2. Year 1 net = 32,000 × 0.75 × 0.80 = 19,200. Year 2 = 19,200 × 0.90 = 17,280.

3. Discounting: 19,200 / 1.035 = 18,550.72 ; 17,280 / 1.035² = 16,131.06 → PV = 34,681.79.

4. NPV = 34,681.79 − 50,000 = −15,318.21. SROI = 34,681.79 / 50,000 = 0.69 : 1.

5. Interprétation: each €1 invested yields only about €0.69 of discounted social value over the two years considered: with these assumptions the programme does not recover its cost. A sensitivity analysis (e.g. longer duration, lower deadweight) would show how robust this conclusion is.

Exercice d’entraînement généré

D — Hutchinson et al. check

In Hutchinson et al. (2020), the mental health proxy is AUD 3,048.75; weighting 75%, deadweight 80%, attribution 60%. (a) Compute the year-1 social value. (b) Year 2 with a 2.5% discount.

Voir la correction

(a) 3,048.75 × 0.75 × 0.80 × 0.60 = AUD 1,097.55 (value in the article). (b) 1,097.55 × (1 − 0.025) = AUD 1,070.11 (value in the article).

Note: the article treats 0.8 and 0.6 as the values multiplied directly (its wording defines deadweight 80% as “outcome would not have happened without the vehicle”, i.e. the share kept), unlike the course table where deadweight is the share deducted. Watch which convention is used.
