Idée centrale

Sustainability must be aligned with positioning, value proposition, resources and every activity of the value chain. A “sustainable” product often fails because the management system (evaluation, bonuses, spare parts) pushes the opposite way.

01DéfinitionStrategic alignment and sustainability

Strategic alignment (sustainable innovation)

Ensuring that an organisation’s environmental and social objectives are consistent with its competitive positioning, its value proposition, its resources and capabilities, and all activities across its value chain.

This alignment helps create a sustainable competitive advantage.

Situation typique (slide 22)

“We want to develop sustainable and repairable products.” But:

  • Purchasing staff are assessed solely on price.
  • Engineers are assessed on time-to-market.
  • Sales staff get bonuses on the number of new products sold.
  • Spare parts are not available.

→ The “sustainable” product conflicts with the company’s management system.

02OutilPorter’s value chain

Value chain

The various stages through which an organisation creates value for its customers (Johnson et al., 2011).

Core (primary) functions — directly linked to value creationSupport functions — improve effectiveness and efficiency of core functions
Procurement · Production · Logistics · Marketing · Services
(Porter diagram: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service)		Technological development · Human resources management · Infrastructure
(Porter diagram also shows Procurement among support activities)

Écart de classification

The text slide lists Procurement as a core function, but the Porter diagram (and the Apple example, “Buying Department” under Support Activities) puts it in the support activities. Both appear in the slides.

What value-chain analysis is used for

  1. Describe the company’s operations and activities.
  2. Understand the functions through which the company creates value for customers/stakeholders. Does it have a firm grasp of the areas where it creates value?
  3. Understand which functions create little value but cost money. Can it reduce costs/margins without risk? Can it externalize the function without risk?

Exemple du cours — Apple’s value chain

FunctionFacts in the slides
Supply & productionSourcing from several countries/continents; pressure to manufacture fast (risk of obsolescence); seeks new subcontractors for its CSR policy (more recycled materials); production line in China to minimise costs, then shipped to the Californian warehouse

Buying (support)200 suppliers in 2021; win-win relationship; a supplier not meeting specifications is immediately replaced by a competitor; long-term partnership that contributes directly to R&D and marketing (at a cost)

Exemple du cours — IKEA (session 3 link)

Moving from a value chain organised around selling new furniture to one that emphasises product lifespan, repair, reuse and material recovery requires transforming the whole value creation system, not just designing greener furniture (see chapter 3).

Tech-Cleaning: colour touchscreen; Wi-Fi + mobile app; 18 programmes; automatic detergent dispensing; load sensors; steam programme; variable-speed motor; proprietary electronic components; sophisticated design; 2-year warranty; €849.

Information absente

The slides ask to (1) outline the value chain, (2) propose a more frugal solution, (3) list the questions to transform the value chain, but give no definition of “frugal innovation” and no model answer. Nothing is invented here. A practice way to approach it is to apply the value-chain questions above (which activities create little value but cost money? where does the management system conflict with repairability/durability?).

04OutilBusiness Model Canvas (BMC)

BMC

A visual tool used in management and entrepreneurship to describe, design, analyse and concisely represent a company’s business model. Developed by Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur (“Business Model Generation”). A sheet/table divided into nine blocks, best examined in order. The creative process is value-driven: how does value benefit customers (and other stakeholders)?

#BlockSlide definition
1Customer segmentstarget users/buyers of your product/service

5Revenue streamsincome-generating model (subscription plans, direct sales…)

05Article obligatoireAcosta et al. (2014): sustainable business models

Article: Acosta, Acquier, Carbone et al. (2014), Les business models du développement durable, L’Expansion Management Review 152(1), 20-29.

  • Objective: how businesses reconcile environmental and social challenges with economic activity.
  • Data: 2013 study (ESCP Europe i7 with weave AIR): interviews with 34 European executives (multinationals to start-ups).

Main results

  • Sustainable innovation is not a compromise but an opportunity to optimise economic, social and environmental aspects simultaneously.
  • It must go beyond continuous improvement or sector conventions: a forward-looking strategic vision.
  • Main condition: a fundamental overhaul of the way we innovate and manage innovation to build a sustainable and viable business model.
