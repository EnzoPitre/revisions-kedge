A — Full capital budgeting case

A firm buys a machine for $30,000, depreciated 5 years straight-line. After 3 years (end of the project) it is sold for $18,000. The project adds 8,000 units/year at $6 per unit; variable cost $3.50 per unit; fixed costs $4,000/year. Investment in net working capital: $8,000 (recovered at the end). Tax rate 34%, required return 15%, project life 3 years. Compute the cash flows, the NPV and the IRR. Should the firm invest?

Exercice d’entraînement généré

B — Selling an old asset (replacement)

An old machine has a book value of $10,000. It can be sold for (a) $6,000 or (b) $13,000. Tax rate 34%. What is the cash flow from the sale in each case?

Voir la correction

(a) Selling price < book value: tax concession = (10,000 − 6,000) × 34% = 1,360; CF = 6,000 + 1,360 = $7,360.

(b) Selling price > book value: tax = (13,000 − 10,000) × 34% = 1,020; CF = 13,000 − 1,020 = $11,980.

Interprétation : selling at a loss gives a tax saving (more cash than the price); selling at a gain triggers a tax (less cash than the price).

Exercice d’entraînement généré

C — From OCF to free cash flow

A firm has OCF = 500, capital expenditures = 120 and a change in net working capital of 30. Compute the free cash flow. Then compute it again if net working capital falls by 30 instead.

Voir la correction

Formule : FCF = OCF − Capex − ΔNWC.

Case 1 (ΔNWC = +30): FCF = 500 − 120 − 30 = 350.

Case 2 (ΔNWC = −30): FCF = 500 − 120 − (−30) = 410.

Interprétation : an increase in working capital uses cash; a decrease releases it.

Exercice d’entraînement généré

D — CFO with the indirect method

Net income 80; depreciation 30; gain on sale of land 5; increase in accounts receivable/inventory 12; increase in payables 4. Compute CFO.

Voir la correction

Formule du cours : CFO = Net income + Depreciation − Non-op. gains + Non-op. losses − ΔOperating assets + ΔOperating liabilities.

CFO = 80 + 30 − 5 − 12 + 4 = 97.

Interprétation : the gain on land is non-operating (removed), the increase in receivables/inventory absorbs cash, the increase in payables brings cash.
