Exercise 1 — Baseline decision and discount-rate sensitivity

Initial outlay 230, then cash flows for years 1–10: 23, 12, 34, 45, 50, 50, 50, 50, 50, 50. (a) PV of each CF, NPV at 6% and IRR; accept? (b) NPV at 8%, 10%, 12%; what changes between 10% and 12%? (c) How does the IRR relate to (b)?

Voir la correction

(a) PV of CFs at 6%: 21.70 ; 10.68 ; 28.55 ; 35.64 ; 37.36 ; 35.25 ; 33.25 ; 31.37 ; 29.59 ; 27.92. Sum = 291.32, minus 230 → NPV = 61.32, IRR = 10.5% (corrigé du fichier).

Decision: accept — NPV > 0 and IRR (10.5%) > 6%.

(b) NPV at 8% = 31.55 ; at 10% = 5.84 ; at 12% = −16.46. Between 10% and 12% the NPV becomes negative (from 5.84 to −16.46).

(c) IRR ≈ 10.5%, so the NPV becomes zero between 10% and 12%: above the IRR the NPV is negative.

Exercise 4 — Break-even outlay and Goal Seek

Same project as Exercise 1 (6%); only the initial investment changes. (a) Maximum outlay (NPV = 0)? (b) Supplier offers 200 instead of 230: new NPV and IRR. (c) Why does a 30 reduction raise the NPV by exactly 30 but the IRR by only about 2.9 points?

Voir la correction

(a) PV of years 1–10 = 291.32 → maximum outlay = 291.32 (calculé) (Goal Seek on NPV = 0 gives the same).

(b) NPV = 291.32 − 200 = 91.32 (original: 61.32). IRR: 10.50% → 13.37%; change = +30 in NPV and about +2.87 points of IRR.

(c) The NPV is in money units: lowering the outlay by 30 at time 0 adds exactly 30 (time 0 is not discounted). The IRR is a rate: a 30 saving on 230 changes the return on the later cash flows only moderately, hence ≈2.9 points.

Exercise 5 — The vineyard: plant now (A) or wait (B)?

Discount rate 7%. Plan A (15 periods): −500 (year 0), −40 (years 1–2), 30 (y3), 80 (y4), 120 (y5), then 150 a year years 6–15; nothing after. Plan B: −600 in year 2, −40 in years 3–4, then 40, 120, 180 (y5–7), 220 a year (y8–10); afterwards a perpetuity of 60 a year from year 11. (a) NPV and IRR of A. (b) For B: terminal value at year 10, its PV, NPV of B (total), and IRR with TV in year 10. (c) Which plan? NPV of B with explicit years only? (d) NPVs at 5, 9, 10, 12%: where does the ranking reverse?

Voir la correction

(a) NPV(A) at 7% ≈ 349.92; IRR(A) ≈ 13.24% (calculé).

(b) Terminal value at year 10 of the perpetuity = 60 / 0.07 ≈ 857.14; PV today = 857.14 / 1.0710 ≈ 435.73. NPV of B with explicit years 0–10 only ≈ −7.11; NPV of B total ≈ −7.11 + 435.73 = 428.62. IRR of B (TV added to year 10 CF: 220 + 857.14) ≈ 16.27%.

(c) At 7%, Plan B (428.62 > 349.92). But B’s explicit years alone have a negative NPV (−7.11): all of B’s value comes from the perpetuity assumption (435.73 of PV), so the choice is very sensitive to that assumption.

Exercice d’entraînement généré

A — Four criteria on one project

Investment 1,200 at t=0; CFs: 300, 400, 500, 600 (years 1–4). Required return 8%. Compute NPV, IRR, PI, payback and discounted payback.

Voir la correction

NPV = 300/1.08 + 400/1.08² + 500/1.08³ + 600/1.08⁴ − 1,200. PVs: 277.78 ; 342.94 ; 396.92 ; 441.02 → sum 1,458.65 → NPV ≈ 258.65 > 0.

IRR (Excel, range incl. t=0) ≈ 16.28% > 8% → accept.

PI = 1 + 258.65/1,200 ≈ 1.22 > 1 → accept.

Payback: cumulative CFs 300, 700, 1,200 → recovered exactly at the end of year 3 → 3.0 years.

Discounted payback: cumulative PVs 277.78 ; 620.71 ; 1,017.63 ; 1,458.65 → remainder after year 3 = 1,200 − 1,017.63 = 182.37 → 3 + 182.37/441.02 ≈ 3.41 years.

Interprétation : le projet est accepté par NPV, IRR et PI ; le discounted payback (3.41) est plus long que le payback (3.0) car il tient compte de la valeur temps.

Exercice d’entraînement généré

B — Limited budget (weighted average PI)

Budget 160. X: investment 100, NPV 30. Y: investment 60, NPV 24. Z: investment 50, NPV 10. Which combination?

Voir la correction

PI = 1 + NPV/Investment: X = 1.30 ; Y = 1.40 ; Z = 1.20. Possible combinations within 160: XY (160), XZ (150), YZ (110), alone X / Y / Z.

WAPI (unused money at PI = 0, as in the course):

  • WAPI(XY) = (1.30×100 + 1.40×60)/160 = 214/160 ≈ 1.34
  • WAPI(XZ) = (130 + 1.20×50)/160 = 190/160 ≈ 1.19
  • WAPI(YZ) = (84 + 60)/160 = 0.90

Résultat : choisir X et Y (WAPI le plus élevé).

Exercice d’entraînement généré

C — IRR rule on a one-year project

Cost 800 today, sale for 900 in one year; foregone investment opportunity 10%. Accept?

Voir la correction

Return = (900 − 800)/800 = 12.5% (same method as the course’s office-building example). 12.5% > 10% → accept by the IRR rule.

Check with NPV: 900/1.10 − 800 = 818.18 − 800 = +18.18 > 0 → same decision.
