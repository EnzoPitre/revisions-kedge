Idée centrale

There is no view from nowhere. Every claim about truth, the good and the just comes from a worldview. Philosophy lets you name which one and choose. Every managerial act assumes one: name it, and judge by it.

01IntroductionWhy philosophy for managers

Where we left off: Session 3 revealed that every economic school conceals a vision of the human being and the good life. Today: from each discipline’s assumptions down to their philosophical roots (“philosophy is where the assumptions are”).

Opening — the 5 “whys”

Take an “obvious” management truth (a good manager is rational and data-driven · the customer is always right · competition makes us better). Keep asking “why?” like a four-year-old (or Socrates). Within 4 or 5 whys, the obvious dissolves into a value choice. That dissolving is philosophy: not abstraction but the discipline of seeing the assumptions you stand on.

3 reasons it matters

  1. Every decision rests on unexamined assumptions about truth, the good and the just. Surface them and you decide better.
  2. The hardest problems aren’t technical but philosophical: what is fair? what do we owe people? what is the point?
  3. Philosophy gives the markers to situate any idea (“markers, not slogans”).

Key questions (the spine): What is truth? (epistemology, knowledge & reality) · What is a good life? (ethics: virtue, joy, meaning) · What is a just decision? (moral & political philosophy).

02Partie 1What is philosophy?

“The love of wisdom”: the disciplined activity of questioning foundations.

Socrates — the founding gesture

Invented the method: relentless questioning that exposes how uncertain our “knowledge” is. Stance: “I know that I know nothing” — wisdom begins in acknowledged ignorance. Tools: irony and maieutics (helping others give birth to their own ideas). Philosophy is not answers but a practice of examination. Hook: the best strategic question is often the naïve one: “why do we actually do it this way?”

DomainQuestions
MetaphysicsWhat is real? What exists?
EpistemologyWhat is truth? What can we know? How do we know it?
EthicsWhat is a good life? What is good, virtuous, worth living for?
PoliticalWhat is a just decision? How should we live together?

Use it: most meetings stall by confusing a question of facts (epistemology) with a question of values (ethics).

Beyond doxa: philosophy distrusts doxa (opinion, the “self-evident”) and demands reasoned argument. “Everyone knows that…” is where philosophy starts. The goal is not cynicism but clarity: holding beliefs you have examined. Hook: reasoned doubt is not indecision, it is the prerequisite of a decision you can defend.

Cas 1 — The data says “yes” (pas de corrigé)

AI selects employees for promotion; the model predicts with 82% accuracy who will perform best. Candidate A has the highest score. Candidate B scores lower, but colleagues describe her as more trusted, courageous and able to bring people together. Who should be promoted? No official answer; link with Aristotle (character, phronēsis) and Habermas.

Part 2 takeaway

There is no view from nowhere. Every claim about truth, the good and the just comes from a worldview. Philosophy lets you name which one and choose.

04Partie 3Philosophy and management

“Every managerial act smuggles in a philosophy of rationality, the good, and legitimacy.”

Habermas — communicative rationality

Legitimacy and truth emerge from open, undistorted dialogue among equals (the “ideal speech situation”), where only the force of the better argument should prevail. A decision gains legitimacy by being optimal and by being reached through fair deliberation. Hook: a choice people helped shape works; one imposed by “the numbers” breeds quiet resistance.

3 lenses for a just decisionQuestionFocus
Virtue (Aristotle)What would a person of good character do here, with phronēsis?character & integrity
Duty (Kant)What rights & duties are at stake? Could I universalize it? Am I treating people as ends?rules & dignity
Consequences (Mill)Which option produces the most overall good for all affected?outcomes & welfare

Robust test

A strong decision survives all three lenses. If they conflict, you’ve found the real dilemma and not avoided it.

Exemple du cours — the trolley and the boardroom

A runaway trolley will kill five; pulling a lever kills one instead. Utilitarian: pull it, five lives outweigh one, maximize total welfare. Kantian: you may not use a person merely as a means, even to save five; duty constrains the calculus. Swap in a real call (a product recall, a layoff, using customer data): same structure, and the frameworks disagree.

Levinas — the face of the Other

Ethics is first philosophy. It begins not in calculation but in the encounter with the face of the Other, whose vulnerability summons an almost infinite responsibility before any rule or contract. Adds relationship and vulnerability: behind every “stakeholder” is a concrete face. Hook: before the spreadsheet of stakeholders, there is a real person affected: start there.

Mini-case — the plant (pairs, 5 min; pas de corrigé)

Cut 40 jobs now or risk 400 in two years. A: cut 40 now → > 75% probability the plant survives. B: keep everyone → > 30% probability the plant closes within two years. C: reduce everyone’s salary by 8% → no layoffs, but employees not consulted. Run it through virtue, duty, consequences; then Levinas: whose face are you not looking at? Which lens wins and why?

Arendt — power, authority, violence

  • Power: the human ability to act in concert.
  • Authority: recognized legitimacy that commands without coercion or argument.
  • Violence: merely instrumental; a sign that power has failed.

A leader who must rely on threats has lost authority; real power is collective, not coercive. Hook: if you can only get compliance by fear or sanction, you have force, not authority — and force is brittle.

Cas 2 — The CEO who is right (pas de corrigé)

Serious strategic crisis; the CEO proposes a radical restructuring. Independent analysis says it is the best economic option, but 70% of employees oppose it. The Board backs the CEO, who says: “Leadership means making the right decision, even when people don’t understand it yet.” Is the CEO a strong leader or an authoritarian one? (Use Arendt and Habermas to reason.)

The philosophical compass

  • Truth: What do I actually know?
  • Values: What do I believe should matter?
  • Ethics: Who could be harmed?
  • Legitimacy: Who has the right to decide?
  • Power: Why will others accept the decision?

The enlightened manager can name the worldview inside any claim and decide whether to endorse it. These markers let you situate ideas and decisions instead of being carried by them.

Spine, exit ticket, next stage

Philosophy questions the foundations → it answers three questions (truth, the good life, the just) → the great traditions are rival worldviews on each → every management act assumes one: name it, and judge by it.

Exit ticket: AI can optimize any answer inside a model; it cannot ask whether the model is true, good, or just. That is you.
