AI vs Human Judgment & Introduction to Sociology

Sociology of Social Transformations & Management · Session 1

13 minFicheWhy the humanities matter in the age of AI, the founders of sociology, and the two opposing schools (holism vs individualism).

Idée centrale

AI does not make humans obsolete: it raises the price of the skills it cannot perform (judgment, interpretation, meaning, responsibility). To resist cognitive delegation you need a discipline that trains you to question the obvious: sociology.

01ContexteCourse set-up & exam
  • Exam: case studies & group works 40% (continuous collective control: group case study in the last session) · individual exam 60% (multiple-choice questionnaire on Learn).
  • 5 sessions: 1 Introduction & Sociology · 2 Anthropology · 3 Economics · 4 Philosophy · 5 Final session.
  • Aim: form enlightened managers capable of critical distance, not technicians. The humanities are “the discipline of judgment”: the trained capacity to step back, interpret and decide well.
  • Intelligence (slide etymology): inter = between, legere = to choose/pick/gather/read → the ability to “choose between”: sort information, make connections/distinctions, tell true from false and important from unimportant.

Questions to keep in mind (slide 11)

  • What intelligence cannot AI replace?
  • Why do democracies need critical thinking?
  • What does sociology let us see that dashboards do not?

Opening question (slide 8): “Where else do we already trust systems we don’t understand?”

5 transformations (not one)

Technological (AI, data, automation) · Environmental (climate, water, resources) · Geopolitical (fragmentation, tensions) · Economic (inflation, supply chains, volatility) · Societal (meaning, work, identity).

3 pressures of our era

  • Rise of AI: authority shifts from people to systems.
  • Acceleration: speed crowds out reflection and meaning.
  • Fragile debate: shared facts and reasoned argument are under strain.
02ConceptsEpisteme, technē, phronēsis, praxis
TermCourse definition
EpistemeUniversal, theoretical knowledge; science and its laws
TechnēCraft and know-how, the “how”. AI scales this almost infinitely
PhronēsisPractical wisdom: deliberating well and acting rightly in a singular situation. AI cannot supply it
PraxisPractical application of a theory, skill or idea, as distinguished from the theory itself

Wager of the course: a business school excels at technē; the humanities cultivate the phronēsis AI cannot replace. “Techniques answer how. The humanities answer why.”

Exemple du cours — New York, 2023

A lawyer filed a brief written with an AI chatbot citing six prior decisions. None existed (hallucination); no human checked; he was sanctioned. The text was fluent, confident and plausible; what was missing was human judgment: “is this true?”

Exemple du cours — Zillow Offers (2021)

An algorithm priced and bought homes at scale and optimized its target (predicted price) almost perfectly; nobody asked if it was the right target as the market turned. Zillow overpaid for thousands of homes, took a ~$500M write-down and shut the unit.

Exemple du cours — Air Canada chatbot (2024)

The chatbot invented a bereavement-fare refund. The airline argued the bot was “responsible for its own actions”; a tribunal held the airline liable: a company owns whatever its systems tell customers.

Exemple du cours — HFT on Euronext

Algorithms trade in microseconds; “flash” events erase value in minutes before a human can grasp what happened.

3 classic managerial mistakes

  1. Confusing data with decision-making
  2. Optimising the wrong problem
  3. Delegating judgement

Hook: the real skill is knowing which task is the machine’s and which is yours.

Question to bear in mind (slide 25)

“In your future role, what will you refuse to delegate?” (no answer given in the course)

Cas de classe 1 — The AI CEO (pas de corrigé dans le cours)

AI recommends closing a French plant and moving production to Asia (predicts €18m annual savings, +2.5 margin points; flags 420 jobs eliminated, higher transport emissions, geopolitical and supply-chain risks). Questions: what can AI calculate? What can it not decide? What information is missing? Who is responsible? Key line: the numbers are AI’s; the decision and its consequences are yours.

Cas de classe 2 — L’Oréal candidate (pas de corrigé)

L’Oréal uses AI tools including Seedlink (tested in China): candidates answer open questions, the algorithm assesses fit with desired competencies. AI does not make the final hiring decision; the recruiter does (quoted source: Eva Azouley, 2019, HR VP / Talent Acquisition director, L’Oréal). Questions on bias, override, accountability, and “what does responsible judgment actually mean?”.

04SociologieBirth and founders of sociology

Why sociology appeared in the 19th century: Industrial Revolution (urbanization, factory labour, new classes) + Democratic Revolution (old order of estates collapses; equality becomes the horizon) → new question: what holds a society together once tradition and hierarchy no longer do? Sociology = “the science that makes the familiar strange and frees us from the obvious” (emancipation from obviousness).

AuthorCore ideaManagerial hook
ComteCreated the word “sociology”; founded positivism (study society with the rigour of natural science). Law of three stages: theological → metaphysical → positive (scientific)Manage society and firms with evidence, not dogma
TocquevilleDemocracy in America: irreversible rise of equality of conditions; new risk = individualism (retreat into private life)Disengaged individualism is an organizational risk (“quiet quitting”)
MarxHistory driven by class struggle; economic infrastructure (relations of production) shapes the superstructure (law, politics, ideas); alienation; ideology makes an order look natural and eternal. Ancestor of the structural campAsk whose interests a “neutral” corporate norm serves
DurkheimKey concepts of the course: solidarity, the collective consciousness and anomie. Social facts = ways of acting/thinking external to individuals that exert constraint (language, law, morality); method: “treat social facts as things”. Mechanical → organic solidarity (likeness → interdependence/division of labour). Anomie (a nomos) and Suicide (1897)Anomie explains crises in firms after brutal change

Part 2 takeaway: a firm is a society in miniature (values, norms, rituals, hierarchies, insiders/outsiders, myths and taboos).

05DistinctionTwo opposing schools

Central question: to explain action, start from society or from the individual? Root: medieval dispute realism (general categories like “society”, “the firm” really exist) vs nominalism (Ockham: only individuals exist; collective names are labels). Holism is heir to realism; methodological individualism heir to nominalism. Hook: is “the company” a real actor, or just a name for people acting? Your answer shapes your management.

School A — Holism / DeterminismSchool B — Individualism / Comprehension
Motto“Society shapes the individual.” To understand the part, look at the whole“Start from the actor.” Look at the parts and the meanings & reasons behind their actions
AuthorsDurkheim, Bourdieu, Dumont, Lévi-Strauss, AlthusserWeber, Simmel, Boudon, Crozier & Friedberg
StrengthExposes power, inequality and the hidden weight of the socialRestores the actor, meaning, strategy and possibility of change; great for organizational analysis
CritiqueOver-determinism: individuals as puppets; social change hard to explainCan underestimate inherited structures and inequality (not everyone starts the “rational choice” from the same place)

School A — authors

  • Durkheim: society is sui generis, a reality greater than the sum of individuals; the individual is largely a product of society (purest holist program).
  • Althusser: ideological state apparatuses (school, family, media) “interpellate” individuals into the roles the system needs.
  • Lévi-Strauss: structuralism: beneath conscious behaviour lie unconscious structures (as in language).
  • Bourdieu: habitus = durable dispositions (tastes, postures, speech) inherited from our trajectory, “structure made body”, generating choices that feel free; action unfolds in a field (structured arena with stakes and rules where agents compete for position); society structured by unequal distribution of capital: economic, cultural (codes, diplomas), social (networks), symbolic (prestige). Hook: “gut feel” in hiring is often habitus recognizing its own kind.
  • Dumont: Homo Hierarchicus (values the whole and hierarchy; India) vs Homo Aequalis (values individual and equality; modern West). Holism vs individualism is a civilizational choice of values; the modern West is unusual in placing the individual above the whole. Hook: “the individual first” is itself a cultural value, not a universal default.

School B — authors

  • Weber: society is fabricated with oriented, meaningful interaction, not external constraint alone. Hook (slide 34): bureaucracy is efficient and dehumanizing; the same overtime can mean exploitation or vocation.
  • Simmel: forms of sociation; society = ongoing web of interactions (conflict, exchange, the secret, “the stranger”, how money and the metropolis reshape the self).
  • Boudon: methodological individualism: explain collective phenomena by aggregating individuals’ (boundedly) rational choices; signature = perverse/unintended effects (traffic jams, bank runs, credential inflation). The whole is not the sum of intentions. Hook: most “irrational” organizational messes are rational individual choices colliding.
  • Crozier & Friedberg (L’acteur et le système, 1977): even in the most constraining organization actors keep a margin of freedom; they pursue strategies and exploit zones of uncertainty as sources of power (whoever controls it has power). Organizations are not machines executing rules but systems of strategic interaction — “the most directly managerial idea in the course”. Hook: to change an organization, map each actor’s strategy, not just the org chart.

Exemple du cours — France Télécom

Brutal restructuring plan (2008–09) linked to a wave of employee suicides; in 2019 the company and executives were convicted of institutional moral harassment. Holist reading: despair traced not to personal fragility but to a structure engineered to push people out.

Exemple du cours — Perverse incentives

A sales force paid purely on volume floods customers with products they will return. Each person acts rationally; the collective result harms the firm (Boudon; no structure “forced” it).

The diagnostic habit — use both grids

Holist question: what structures, norms, inherited dispositions and power relations produce this? (risk: individuals as puppets). Individualist question: what meanings, strategies and rational choices of the actors produce this? (risk: underestimating inherited inequality). Ask it twice; the richest analysis lives in the tension between the grids.

Cas de classe 3 — Why are people leaving? (pas de corrigé)

Consulting firm, 28% annual turnover among young employees. Manager: “Generation Z lacks resilience and loyalty.” Bad news: “The organization is exhausting and meaningless.” Who is right: management, employees, both or neither? Apply the holist and individualist readings.
