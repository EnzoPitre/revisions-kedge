Idée centrale

Capital budgeting = decision process corporations use when they invest capital in new ventures: the comparison of costs versus future profits (advertising campaign, R&D, acquiring a competitor…).

01MéthodeCriteria & standard steps

Criteria most often used: NPV, IRR, Profitability Index (PI), Payback Period (“not so good”), Discounted Payback Period.

Standard steps

  1. Estimate CFs (inflows & outflows).
  2. Assess riskiness of CFs.
  3. Determine the cost of capital i for the project.
  4. Find NPV and/or IRR and/or PI.
  5. Accept if NPV > 0 and/or IRR > WACC and/or PI > 1.
02CritèreNet Present Value (NPV)

Definition

NPV = Present Value minus the monetary value of the initial investment (“Net” because the initial investment is taken into account). For a project: the value of the income generated in excess of the initial investment (cost of the project). The discount rate i is generally the cost of capital appropriate for the company and its project.

Formula

\[ NPV = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+i)^t} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+i)^t} + CF_0 \]

\(i\) = discount rate · \(CF_t\) = cash flow at time t · \(CF_0\) = initial investment (usually a negative number)

NPV = PV inflows − PV of cost = net gain in wealth.

Decision rule

  • NPV > 0: PV of the income stream is greater than the cost → accept (above-normal profits).
  • NPV < 0: cost is greater than the income stream → reject.

Properties: the most used criterion · considers the time value of money · absolute measure of wealth (positive NPVs increase the owner’s wealth, negative ones decrease it) · discount rate = cost of capital · project CFs are assumed reinvested at the cost of capital.

How a firm can generate above-normal profits: buyers’ preference for established brand names · control of distribution systems · patent control · exclusive ownership of superior natural resources · access to lower-cost financial resources · economies of scale · access to superior labor or management talents.

Exemple du cours — Project A, WACC = 10%

CFs: −100 (t=0), 10, 60, 80. PVs: 9.09 + 49.59 + 60.11 = 118.79 (rounded), so NPVA ≈ 18.79 → positive → accept. (Unrounded: 18.78.)

Excel trap (from the exercises’ tip)

NPV(rate, range) discounts the first value by one period. So write =NPV(rate, CF1:CFn)+CF0. IRR needs the full range including time 0.

03NotionNPV and the discount rate

The higher the discount rate, the smaller the NPV (everything else equal); the smaller the rate, the larger the NPV.

04CritèreInternal Rate of Return (IRR)

Definition

The IRR is the rate at which the NPV is zero: the break-even point (expected cash flows cover exactly the cost of the project, considering the time value of money).

Formula

\[ NPV = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+IRR)^t} = 0 \]

Excel: IRR (French version: TRI).

Decision rule

Accept if IRR > discount rate (also called required return, cost of capital or “hurdle rate”). Intuition: the expected return exceeds the required rate of return.

  • In investment projects we speak of required return to stress that investors demand it (chapter 5, WACC).
  • IRR usually gives the same decision as NPV.
  • Limit: for purely algebraic reasons, solving for the IRR may lead to several solutions. Conclusion of the course: it is better to use the NPV rule.

Exemple du cours — office building (foregone opportunity 14%)

Cost \(C_0\) = $370m; sale in year 1 \(C_1\) = $420m. Return = (420,000 − 370,000)/370,000 = 0.135 = 13.5%. It is below 14%, so by the IRR rule the project is not accepted (the notes show the computation, not the written verdict).

Exemple du cours — investment project

NPV = 52.07 (positive → accept). IRR = 9.49% > 7% required → green-lighted: the project gives more income than its cost. The NPV graph shows NPV > 0 for rates below 9.49% (project accepted) and NPV < 0 above (rejected).

05CritèreProfitability Index (PI)

Formula

\[ PI = 1 + \frac{NPV}{\text{Investment}} \]

  • Accept a project if PI > 1; do not accept if PI < 1.
  • When resources are limited, PI helps select among project combinations: the highest weighted average PI indicates which projects to select.

06CritèrePayback & discounted payback

Payback period

The number of years required to recover a project’s cost: how long does it take to get the business’s money back?

Computation

Payback = last full period where accumulated cash flows are still below the initial investment + last (partial) period.

Partial period = (remainder still to recover) / (next cash flow).

Remarque

The notes word the partial period as “next cash flow / remainder”, but all their examples compute remainder ÷ next cash flow (30/80, 30/50). Use the examples’ version.

Exemple du cours — Project B (CFs mostly in later years)

CFs: −100, 10, 60, 80 · cumulative: −100, −90, −30, +50 → payback = 2 + 30/80 = 2.375 years (shown as 2.4).

Exemple du cours — Project C (CFs come quickly)

CFs: −100, 70, 50, 20 · cumulative: −100, −30, +20, +40 → payback = 1 + 30/50 = 1.6 years.

StrengthsWeaknesses
Gives an indication of a project’s risk and liquidity; easy to calculate and understandIgnores the time value of money; ignores CFs occurring after the payback period

Discounted payback

Same method but with discounted rather than raw CFs.

Exemple du cours — Project A at 10%

CFs −100, 10, 60, 80 → PV: −100, 9.09, 49.59, 60.11 · cumulative: −100, −90.91, −41.32, +18.79 → discounted payback = 2 + 41.32/60.11 = 2.7 years. Reading: investment and capital costs recovered in 2.7 years.

Capital budgeting
Decision process for investing capital in new ventures: comparison of costs versus future profits.
NPV (definition)
Present value of the cash flows minus the initial investment.
NPV formula
Σ (t=0..n) CF_t / (1+i)^t
NPV decision rule
NPV > 0: accept. NPV < 0: reject.
What does the discount rate do to NPV?
Higher rate → smaller NPV.
IRR (definition)
The rate at which NPV = 0 (break-even point).
IRR decision rule
Accept if IRR > discount rate (required return / hurdle rate / WACC).
Main limit of IRR
May give several solutions (algebraic) → better to use NPV.
Profitability Index
PI = 1 + NPV / Investment. Accept if PI > 1.
When is PI especially useful?
When resources are limited: choose the combination with the highest weighted average PI.
Payback period
Years needed to recover the project’s cost.
Payback weaknesses
Ignores TVM; ignores CFs after the payback period.
Payback strengths
Indicates risk and liquidity; easy to calculate.
Discounted payback
Payback computed with discounted cash flows.
Excel NPV formula
=NPV(rate, CF1:CFn) + CF0 (NPV discounts the first value by one period).
